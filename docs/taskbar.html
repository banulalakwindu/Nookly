Your apps

Everything you expect, and a bit more

Pinned and running apps, the way you're used to them, with the little things that make a taskbar quick.

Live previews and peekHover an app for its windows, live. Rest on one to see it on the screen; move away and everything is as it was.
Right-click menusIn the taskbar's own look: recent files, the app's own tasks, run as administrator, open file location, pin, close and end task.
Drag into any orderMove apps along the bar, or in and out of the ⋯ tile. Drop one on another to make a collection.
Progress and badgesDownloads and copies fill a bar under the icon, red for an error and yellow when paused. An app's own badge shows too.
Drop files on appsRest on an app, or a window in its pop-up, to bring it forward and go on into it. Or let go to open the file with it.
Nothing missedAn app that wants you glows. Administrator requests waiting in the background show as a shield, first on the bar.
Hidden iconsEvery app's tray icon in one bubble; left and right clicks work as usual, and it stays open while an icon's menu is.
The bell countsHow many notifications came since you last looked. They're only counted, never read.
Like Windows, tooStart, Search, keyboard language, Wi-Fi, sound and battery, the clock, middle-click for a new window, Show desktop in the far corner.

Every screen

A bar wherever you work

Your other screens get a taskbar too, all in the same small process. To begin with it's just apps and widgets there, because that's what second screens are for.

  • You choose what's on it. Start, Search, hidden icons, the clock, the bell and every widget has its own switch for the other screens.
  • The same apps and order on every bar.
  • Plug in, unplug: bars come and go with the screens.
Your layout

One list, in your order

Everything on the bar is one list you drag into place. Pin things at either end, or let them sit in the middle, which scrolls with the wheel when it's full.

  • It grows and shrinks to fit what's on it, like the notch. With auto-hide off it takes the full width, and maximized windows stop above it.
  • Auto-hide slides it away; the Windows key or the screen's edge brings it out.
  • Hover or click to open things, three sizes, centred or left.
  • The notch's theme or its own: Liquid Glass, every other theme, or your own colours.
Keyboard and screen readers

Works without the mouse

One shortcut puts the keyboard on the taskbar. What it's on is ringed in colour, and its name is given to screen readers.

Get it

Three clicks, and you can always go back

The taskbar comes from inside Nookly, only when you ask for it.

Install NooklyAbout 2 MB, no admin rights needed. Download the installer.
Settings > TaskbarClick the gear in the notch, pick the Taskbar tab, then Download taskbar.
That's itIt switches on by itself. The switch turns it off and Windows' taskbar is back at once; Remove taskbar deletes it.

Good to know

 

Do I need the notch to use the taskbar?

The taskbar belongs to Nookly, but the notch has its own switch: turn it off in Settings > Notch and use only the taskbar, or use both. A widget works on the taskbar even when its panel in the notch is hidden.

Does it change Windows?

No system files are touched. While it runs, Windows' own taskbar is hidden; switch it off, quit Nookly or uninstall it, and Windows' taskbar is as it was. If it ever crashes, Nookly puts Windows' taskbar back by itself.

What about games and full-screen videos?

The bar steps aside for full-screen apps, as Windows' taskbar does.

Is it private?

Yes. No account, no analytics, no ads. Widgets that need the internet (weather, currency, the quote) go online only once you add them. Recent files come from Windows' own list when you open a menu, and follow your Windows privacy setting. Read the privacy policy.

Which Windows does it need?

Windows 10 or 11, as the notch does.
